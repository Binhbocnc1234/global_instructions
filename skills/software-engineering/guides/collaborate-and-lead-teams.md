# Collaborate and Lead Software Teams

## Use When
- Forming, leading, or restructuring a software team.
- Coordinating distributed contributors or resolving communication failure.
- Planning work where staffing, ownership, or decision authority is unclear.

## Required Context
- Product purpose, problem difficulty, schedule pressure, and required expertise.
- Team size, individual strengths, experience, availability, and location.
- Decision rights, ownership boundaries, dependencies, and communication channels.
- Organizational culture, stakeholder access, and sources of conflict.

## Instructions
1. Select people for problem-solving ability, communication, adaptability, and relevant knowledge, not only tool familiarity.
2. Establish shared product goals, quality expectations, priorities, and definition of completion.
3. Choose a team structure that balances control and autonomy:
   - More centralized coordination for tightly constrained or high-risk work.
   - Decentralized collaboration for complex discovery and innovative problem solving.
4. Assign ownership for decisions and interfaces while preserving collective visibility and backup knowledge.
5. Define coordination mechanisms: direct conversations, written decisions, issue tracking, reviews, integration cadence, and escalation paths.
6. Keep teams cohesive and small enough for effective communication; partition larger efforts around clear interfaces and product capabilities.
7. For distributed teams, establish overlapping hours, durable records, shared repositories, response expectations, and culturally explicit communication norms.
8. Protect concentration and sustainable pace; remove blockers and reduce unnecessary context switching.
9. Use reviews, pairing, demonstrations, and retrospectives to spread knowledge and discover both product and process defects.
10. Resolve conflict around evidence, requirements, constraints, and alternatives rather than hierarchy or personality.

## Decision Rules
- If a problem is novel and complex, increase collaboration and team autonomy.
- If coordination overhead exceeds productive work, reduce team size, dependencies, or active work—not merely add meetings.
- If critical knowledge has one owner, add review, pairing, documentation, or rotation before it becomes a continuity risk.
- If a project is late, evaluate dependency and onboarding costs before adding people; additional staff can initially make it later.
- If a communication channel produces ambiguous decisions, move decisions to a durable written artifact.
- If schedule pressure causes persistent overtime, renegotiate scope or schedule because fatigue increases defect and turnover risk.

## Constraints
- Do not evaluate team members using decontextualized code or defect counts.
- Do not equate silence with agreement; solicit competing viewpoints.
- Do not create ownership boundaries that prevent integration responsibility.
- Leadership must facilitate decisions and remove barriers, not hide bad news.

## Failure Modes
- **Hero dependency** — Cause: knowledge and authority concentrated in one person. Correction: pair, review, document, and distribute ownership.
- **Meeting saturation** — Cause: architecture and work dependencies require constant synchronization. Correction: simplify interfaces and reduce concurrent work.
- **Distributed-team drift** — Cause: informal decisions lack durable records. Correction: record decisions, owners, assumptions, and effective dates.
- **Toxic pace** — Cause: unrealistic commitments and chronic interruption. Correction: replan, limit WIP, restore sustainable workload.
- **Conflict becomes personal** — Cause: no shared decision criteria. Correction: return to stakeholder value, constraints, evidence, and trade-offs.

## Verification
- Every critical capability and interface has ownership and backup visibility.
- Decision and escalation mechanisms are explicit.
- Team structure matches problem and coordination needs.
- Workload and WIP are sustainable.
- Retrospectives or postmortems produce assigned changes rather than observations only.
